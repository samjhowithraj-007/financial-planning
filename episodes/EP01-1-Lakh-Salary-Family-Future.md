## 1. Brief

- **Audience problem:** "Salary theek-thaak hai, phir bhi lagta hai future ka koi plan nahi hai. Bachchon ki padhai, ghar, retirement, parents… shuru kahan se karein?"
- **Learning outcome:** Viewers meet the example family, see where ₹1,00,000 goes each month, and learn that **every goal needs its own deadline and its own pot of money**, and that not every goal can be fully funded at once.
- **One useful action:** Write down your family's 5 goals, with the year each one is needed and a rough amount (an estimate is fine).
- **Must not imply:** That ₹31,000 a month of saving already pays for education, a home and retirement in full. Episode 01 only shows the starting point. Later episodes do the gap calculations.

## 6. Talking points

### Point 1 — Meet the Verma family (0:15–1:45)
- Introduce Amit (35), Neha (33), Aarav (8) and Pihu (4) in Lucknow. Rented 2BHK, ₹1,00,000 combined take-home.
- "Ye koi ameer ya gareeb family nahi—bilkul hamari-aapki jaisi family hai." Say clearly that the family is **fictional**, an example to learn from and not an "average Indian family" statistic.
- Their money today: ₹60,000 in savings, Amit's EPF, a company health policy and no loans. "Achhi baat: koi karz nahi. Chinta ki baat: ₹60,000 sirf ek mahine ka zaroori kharch hai."

### Point 2 — Where the ₹1 lakh goes (1:45–3:45)
- Reveal the budget in three blocks (animated stacked bar):
  - **Zaroori kharch ₹58,000 (58%)**: rent, ration, bills, transport, school, health.
  - **Saal bhar ke kharche ₹6,000 + masti ₹5,000**: "Diwali, school admission, shaadi ke gifts—ye 'achaanak' nahi aate, har saal aate hain." (Teaser for Episode 06.)
  - **Future ke liye ₹31,000 (31%)**: emergency ₹8,000, retirement ₹13,000, bachchon ki padhai ₹10,000.
- "31% bachana kaafi achha start hai. Lekin sawaal ye hai: kya ye kaafi hai?"

### Point 3 — Competing goals: everything at once is impossible (3:45–5:45)
- Goal board with deadlines (from the family's point of view):
  1. Emergency fund — **abhi** (now)
  2. Insurance check (health + term) — **abhi**
  3. Aarav's college — **~10 saal** (in about 10 years)
  4. Pihu's college — **~14 saal**
  5. Apna ghar — **"kabhi na kabhi"** (someday; no date yet, and that's the problem)
  6. Retirement — **25 saal**
  7. Parents' care — **kabhi bhi zaroorat pad sakti hai** (could be needed any time)
- The contributions-only numbers: ₹10,000 a month for 10 years = ₹12,00,000 deposited for **both** kids. ₹13,000 a month for 25 years = ₹39,00,000 deposited for retirement. Stress: **"Ye growth se pehle ka number hai, aur hum abhi nahi jaante ki college ya retirement ka asli kharcha kitna hoga. Wo hum Episode 41 aur 46 mein calculate karenge."** No claim that these amounts are enough or not enough.
- Core idea: **har goal ka apna deadline, apna dabba (pot).** Money needed in 1 year and money needed in 25 years should not be planned the same way. (Episode 21 covers this in depth.)
- Priority idea, kept simple: first build safety (emergency fund, insurance, no expensive debt), then long-term goals. Don't make it sound like a fixed rule; say it is the order this series will follow.

### Point 4 — Your one action (5:45–6:40)
- "Aaj ek kaagaz lijiye. Apni family ke 5 goals likhiye. Har goal ke saamne: **kab chahiye** (saal) aur **lagbhag kitna** (andaaza chalega)."
- Show Neha's handwritten sample list on screen.
- "Agar kisi goal ka saal nahi likh paa rahe, toh wo abhi goal nahi, sirf sapna hai, aur sapna bhi theek hai. Bas usse alag likhiye."

### Close (6:40–7:00)
- Next episode: "Salary Aati Hai, Phir Gayab Kahan Ho Jaati Hai?" Track one month of spending.
- CTA: "Comment mein likhiye: aapka sabse bada goal kaunsa hai?" Ask viewers to subscribe for the series.
- Disclaimer (spoken briefly plus on screen): "Ye ek educational example hai, personal financial advice nahi. Apni situation ke hisaab se decide kijiye."
